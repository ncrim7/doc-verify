# Measurement — 2026-09-06 · real-world validation, n=15 (P1-5)

Fifteen real documents, hand-ground-truthed, 202 scored fields. The previous
real figure rested on four documents and 49 fields.

**70.48% overall — and that number is close to useless.** What follows is the
reason.

## Input condition explains almost everything

| condition | EM | n | documents |
|---|---|---|---|
| clean digital PDF | **90.5%** | 8 | 89, 100, 89, 74, 92, 100, 100, 80 |
| screen capture | 88.9% | 1 | |
| photo, crisp thermal print | 85.7% | 1 | |
| PDF, broken or missing text layer | 56.4% | 2 | 55, 58 |
| **photo, dot-matrix on faded thermal** | **15.1%** | 3 | 20, 10, 15 |

A **75-point spread**, driven by how the document arrived and by nothing else.

Two other cuts look dramatic and are confounded, so they should not be quoted:
receipts score 94.3% against invoices at 61.8%, and English 92.7% against
Turkish 62.4% — but three of the four receipts and all four English documents
are clean digital PDFs. Condition is the variable; the others ride on it.

The comparison that matters is inside one format. The three ASAT water bills
are the same issuer, the same layout, the same fields, photographed within
seconds of each other, and they score 20%, 10% and 15%. Meanwhile a phone photo
of a crumpled, stained till receipt scores 85.7%. **It is not "photos are
hard". It is "dot-matrix overprint on faded thermal paper is hard"** — a crisp
offset letterhead over an illegible body, which no prompt work will fix.

## What this means for the product

A bookkeeping office's document mix is not uniform. On e-arşiv PDFs the system
is usable today. On photographed utility bills it is not, and saying so is
worth more than an average that hides both.

The honest sales sentence is two sentences: **90% on clean digital documents,
and utility bills photographed off thermal paper need a different answer** —
a portal integration, an e-arşiv XML feed, or a "re-photograph this" prompt at
capture time. Not a better model.

## The safety net's ceiling, measured

Of 50 field errors, **21 (42%) are in principle catchable** by the verifier —
they sit in fields with internal redundancy: arithmetic (subtotal, tax,
totals, line totals) or a check digit (tax ids). The other **29 (58%) are not
catchable by any rule we could write**: invoice numbers, names, addresses,
dates, descriptions have no internal structure to check them against.

Five errors slipped through on documents the pipeline returned as OK:

```
real_002  invoice_number  1OSB6CRZ-0004  ->  10SB6CRZ-0004      O read as zero
real_007  invoice_number  1OSB6CRZ-0005  ->  10SB6CRZ-0005      the same, again
real_002  buyer_address   Beyazıt … Şeyhan  ->  Beyazit … Seyhan   diacritics dropped
real_009  description     Claude Pro Apr 13–May 13, 2026  ->  Claude Pro
real_013  store_name      SARITEKELİ MARKET  ->  SARITEKEI MARKET
```

The O/0 confusion repeats across two documents of the same format, so it is
systematic rather than random — and it is exactly the class the verifier cannot
see. **This is a structural statement about the architecture, not a defect
list**: rule-based verification catches money and tax ids, and is blind to
identifiers and text. Anything better needs a second source to compare against
— the e-arşiv XML, a supplier master, or a human.

## The verdict was right where it could be

`real_006` came back **REVIEW with a 100% correct extraction**. That is not a
false positive: the document itself carries `11111111111` as the buyer tax id,
and the check digit says so. Perfect reading of a document that contains a
bogus number, correctly escalated.

That placeholder now appears on **three of fifteen documents, from three
independent sellers**. It is a pattern, not a curiosity: a bookkeeping tool
that trusts the buyer tax id printed on a Turkish e-arşiv invoice will post
rubbish about a fifth of the time.

## Two failures worth naming

**`real_014` returned 1017.15 as the invoice total.** The correct figure is
1073.69. 1017.15 is the *overdue balance from an earlier period*, printed at
the foot of the page under `SON ÖDEME TARİHİ GEÇMİŞ BORÇ`. This is the same
trap as `real_004`'s carried-over balance in a different layout, and it is the
most financially dangerous error class in the corpus: a plausible number from
the right document that belongs to a different period.

**`real_015` returned `NAZMI CİRiM` where the page prints `NAZMI CIRIM`.** The
model *invented* Turkish characters that are not there, and got them wrong
while doing it. This is P1-4b confirmed on real data: the prompt fix that
stopped the model stripping diacritics has taught it to add them. The next
prompt iteration has to be symmetric — preserve what is printed, in both
directions.

## Ground truth: what was built and what it refused to assert

15 documents, 206 available fields, 202 of them scored. Every value read either
from an embedded PDF text layer or by eye from the render, never from the
system under test.

Fields were **excluded rather than guessed** wherever a careful human could not
read them. `real_012` gives up its invoice number, buyer name, buyer tax id and
buyer address for exactly this reason: the dot-matrix overprint is illegible at
4x, the name is obvious from context, and inferring it would have put an
invented value into the ground truth. A smaller ground truth is worth more than
a confident wrong one.

Where the schema, not the page, was the problem, that is recorded too.

## Schema gaps the corpus exposed

Every one of these is our limitation, not a model error.

| gap | documents |
|---|---|
| two tax bases on one document (KDV+ÖİV, KDV 0%+20%) | real_004, real_005, real_015 |
| charge lines / consumption tiers with no quantity or unit price | real_004, real_005, real_012, real_013, real_014 |
| no discount field — `quantity × unit_price` cannot reach the line total | real_008 |
| `items[].total` undefined as tax-inclusive or exclusive | real_006, real_015 |
| a payment receipt's link to the invoice it settles | real_009, real_010 |
| a till receipt's fiscal identifiers (VD number, Z No, EKÜ No, MF serial) | real_013 |
| a tax kind alongside the rate — BSMV is not KDV | real_011 |

Three of fifteen documents carry two tax bases and five of fifteen have no
per-line quantity. These are not edge cases; they are the ordinary shape of
Turkish utility and platform invoices, and the schema was drawn from clean
commercial invoices that do not look like them.

## Method notes

The corpus is deliberately uneven and stays that way. It contains near-duplicate
pairs (real_002/real_007, real_009/real_010) as stability probes: the same
format twice should score alike, and a gap between them is variance rather than
difficulty. It spans two years, four document conditions, three tax regimes and
both languages.

`real_005` earns a special mention: its vendor name, address and VKN exist
**only inside a stamp graphic** and are absent from the PDF text layer entirely.
A text-layer extractor returns nothing for three required fields on a page that
reads perfectly to a human. That is the argument for the vision approach stated
as a measurable case rather than an assertion.

## Scope

15 documents is enough to rank input conditions and to name defect classes. It
is not enough to put a confidence interval on any single condition — the
photo group is n=3, the broken-text-layer group n=2. Every figure here is one
run; the synthetic noise floor is σ = 0.178 pp at n=60, and it will be larger
at n=15.
